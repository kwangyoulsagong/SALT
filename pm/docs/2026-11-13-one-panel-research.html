Counter-evidence verdict

Predictability is real, but in every verified case it has one of three shapes:

  • Tiny per-bet edge × large breadth (GKX, JKP, Chen–Zimmermann): R² ≈ 0.4%/month per stock; value comes from ranking thousands of names and trading the tails. Reported gross of costs; publication roughly halves it (McLean–Pontiff 58%).
  • Microstructure horizons (Chinco et al., Aït-Sahalia et al.): accuracy of 64%+ and R² of 2–10% exist at 5 seconds to 1 minute, and vanish with milliseconds of delay. Not available to a retail-latency system.
  • Aggregate market timing with heavy shrinkage (Campbell–Thompson, Rapach et al.): "small but economically meaningful", and the underlying variables mostly failed GWZ's 2022 re-test.

Nothing verified here shows a daily-or-longer directional hit rate far above the low 50s for a broad universe after costs.

Not as a single canonical statistic. It is a rule of thumb, but one that is consistent with several documented numbers. There is no paper I could find whose finding is "direction prediction stalls at 52%". What actually exists:

Headline

  • Direction is barely predictable; volatility and range are strongly predictable. Best-documented short-horizon direction effects have R² of about 1-3% (intraday market momentum) or tens of basis points over days (event drift / reversal). Realized volatility has out-of-sample Mincer-Zarnowitz R² of 0.26-0.70 at one day.
  • Selective prediction's headline accuracy is mostly base rate and persistence. The one paper that checks coverage-matched naive baselines finds a gap of +1.7 to +7.4 pp, with only one of five horizons statistically distinguishable from zero.
  • Korean investor-flow signals look strong in sample and fail out of sample in the only paper found that runs a frozen-parameter test (OOS Sharpe -1.65).
  • Conditional structure (events x news x volume x who is buying) is where the evidence points, which matches the app's own finding that conditional base rates differ from the unconditional rate while the model shows no skill.

Topic 9 verdict. Verified evidence supports three design claims: diversification tools help the under-diversified; salience of purchase price and push alerts worsen behaviour; AI advice is highly persuasive in both directions. There is no verified evidence that showing forecasts raises realized returns. That gap is itself a publishable contribution if the app logs decisions — but outcome claims need randomized exposure.

Ranked shortlist — five hypotheses to preregister with existing data

Effect sizes below are from other markets or samples; they are priors, not expectations the app is entitled to.

H1. Next-day and next-hour realized volatility/range is forecastable, and conformalized intervals hit nominal coverage (stocks and KRW crypto)

  • Test. HAR on 5-minute realized variance (daily, weekly, monthly lags) for 1-hour, 1-day and 5-day horizons; wrap with rolling/adaptive conformal for 90% and 95% return intervals.
  • Support. Corsi 2009 (OOS R² 0.55-0.70 at 1 day for FX and S&P; 0.26 T-bond); Hu et al. (Bitcoin HAR R² about 0.24 in sample); Gibbs-Candès; Aich et al. (coverage 0.9523 / 0.9537 at 95% target vs 0.84-0.86 uncorrected); Eom et al. (range falls about a third in the hour after a dynamic VI — an event feature).
  • Expected. R² 0.2-0.6 depending on asset; coverage within 1 pp of nominal.
  • Honest baseline. Yesterday's realized variance (random walk), EWMA, and historical-simulation intervals; score with QLIKE/MSE, Winkler score, Diebold-Mariano. Beating HAR with ML by more than about 10% is unlikely (Christensen et al.).
  • Main risk. It is not direction; conformal alone lost to volatility-scaled intervals on interval score in 42 of 52 comparisons. The 2015 limit change and VI rules are regime breaks.

H2. The app's conditional base rates, used as selective forecasts, beat the majority class and a coverage-matched naive rule

  • Test. Preregister a small fixed set of conditions (e.g. after a -2 sigma day; after a >10% day with/without disclosure; after concentrated individual buying). Forecast only when a condition fires; report Brier skill vs unconditional rate, accuracy vs majority at the same coverage, and vs the simplest single-condition rule.
  • Support. Chalkidis-Savani (+1.4 to +2.9 pp at 37-63% coverage); Gupta-Liu protocol and warning (gap over persistence +1.7 to +7.4 pp, one of five significant); Giacomini-White for conditional evaluation.
  • Expected. +1 to +3 pp accuracy on covered cases at 10-30% coverage; Brier skill of a few percent.
  • Honest baseline. Majority class on the covered subset, plus coverage-matched naive conditional rule; balanced accuracy; results on sign-change days separately.
  • Main risk. Gains are base-rate artifacts of the covered subset; many conditions -> Holm/BH; low event counts.

H3. After a large one-day move, direction over days 1-5 depends on whether a disclosure/news item accompanied it and on event-day volume (Korean stocks)

  • Test. Event = |daily return| > 10% (or > k sigma). Split by presence of a disclosure title or headline that day, and by abnormal volume. Hypothesis: no-news/high-volume moves reverse over days 1-5; news-accompanied moves drift.
  • Support. Lee-Park-Son 2023 (KOSPI distribution sector: after >10% rises, day 1 +0.916 then days 1-5 -0.372; after falls, days 1-5 +0.779; 7,317 and 3,375 events); Jiang-Li-Wang (news-driven returns drift "for several days ... without reversals"); Ke-Kelly-Xiu (response concentrated in small, volatile stocks); Nagel (reversal stronger in high volatility).
  • Expected. 0.3-0.8% over five days in abnormal return; a few pp shift in up-probability.
  • Honest baseline. The move-only conditional base rate (ignoring news), plus majority class. The claim is the interaction, so the news split must add Brier skill over the move-only rule.
  • Main risk. Day-1 continuation before reversal makes entry timing matter; headline/disclosure presence is a noisy proxy for information; single-sector prior; limit-hit days truncate returns.

H4. Concentrated individual net buying on attention days predicts negative abnormal returns over the next 1-5 days (Korean stocks)

  • Test. Per-stock individual net buying (or minus foreign+institution net buying) scaled by volume, top decile, interacted with abnormal volume / news count. Target: 1-, 3-, 5-day market-adjusted return sign and size.
  • Support. Barber et al. (5-day abnormal return -3%, -6% for extreme herding); Barber-Odean 2008; JKDAS 2024 (individual net buying negatively Granger-causes KOSPI returns, fading by day 3 — abstract level); Kim-Byun 2011 (prior-day foreign/institution buying -> negative next-day close-to-close, positive overnight — abstract level).
  • Expected. Much smaller than the U.S. Robinhood figure; plausibly tens of bps over 3 days.
  • Honest baseline. Short-term reversal alone (prior-return rule) and majority class — flow must add skill beyond the price move it is correlated with.
  • Main risk. Kang 2026: Korean flow signals with t above 25 in sample gave OOS Sharpe -1.65. Flow follows returns (Choe-Kho-Stulz). Preregister with frozen parameters or do not claim it.

H5. Index-level intraday momentum: the return from the previous close to 30 minutes before the close predicts the last 30 minutes (KOSPI 200 / KOSDAQ 150 proxies, and BTC/KRW by volume clock)

  • Test. r(prev close -> 14:50) vs r(14:50 -> 15:20, before the closing auction); also first-30-minute predictor. Report R²_OOS against a zero forecast and sign hit rate against majority.
  • Support. Gao et al. (R² 1.6% in sample, 1.2% out of sample, Sharpe 1.08); Baltussen et al. (60+ futures 1974-2020, equity R² 1.49-2.74%, Sharpe 0.87-1.73; gamma-hedging mechanism); crypto analogue Shen-Urquhart-Wang (search summary only).
  • Expected. R² 0.5-2.5%; hit rate 52-54%.
  • Honest baseline. Zero/prevailing-mean forecast and majority sign; the first-half-hour predictor has negative OOS R² (-1.71%) in the extended sample, so register the rest-of-day version as primary.
  • Main risk. Horizon is 30 minutes, just under the stated 1-hour floor; Korean single-stock evidence (thesis, secondary) points to last-hour reversal; needs an index or ETF 5-minute series; the effect reverts over following days.

Runner-up (crypto)

  • Liquid KRW pairs continue and illiquid pairs reverse at the 1-day horizon (Liu-Tsyvinski 0.33% per SD; Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu 4.2% vs 0.6% weekly by size; Zaremba secondary). Baseline: majority class and unconditional drift. Risk: 2011-2018 evidence, pre-cost, hourly ML fails at 10 bps (Bysik-Ślepaczuk).
  • New KRW listings underperform after day 1 (Four Pillars: mean -69%, median -81%; practitioner, not market-adjusted). Testable only if listing dates are captured; must be benchmarked against an altcoin index.

What is NOT feasible with 5-minute bars and no order book

  • Order-flow imbalance, queue, microprice and depth signals. The magnet-effect evidence (Du-Liu-Rhee) rests on order flow and order type; none of it is reproducible.
  • VI call-auction analysis. Eom et al.'s reversal test uses the potential execution price inside the auction. Only the before/after range can be measured.
  • Sub-5-minute news reaction. Ke-Kelly-Xiu's response is measured in the first 15 minutes after arrival and is largest in small stocks; with 5-minute bars and headline timestamps the app sees at most the tail of it.
  • Full-text sentiment models. KKX-style supervised scoring needs article bodies and a long labelled history; headlines and disclosure titles support only event-presence and coarse category features.
  • Earnings-surprise PEAD. Needs SUE from financial statements or analyst forecasts; disclosure titles do not carry the number.
  • Realistic cost, slippage and capacity estimates. Without quotes there is no spread; any "after-cost" claim would be an assumption. Report gross forecast skill and state the cost assumption separately.
  • Trade-signed or intraday investor-type flow. Daily investor-type totals cannot identify who initiated trades or when.
  • Cross-exchange arbitrage claims (kimchi premium). Needs synchronized offshore prices and FX, and the literature frames it as limits to arbitrage, not return prediction.
  • Any claim that showing forecasts improved users' returns. Needs randomized exposure and outcome tracking; the app records no orders.
  • Tick-level realized measures (jumps, noise-robust estimators). 5-minute realized variance is standard and sufficient; finer jump decomposition is not available.

Conclusions

(a) Ten evidence-backed rules for the one-panel scenario card, ranked

  • State the probability as a number, never as a word alone. Verbal hedges cut trust in the figure (d = 0.75) while a numeric range did not (van der Bles 2020); readers misread probability words even with a legend (Budescu 2009; Dhami & Mandel 2022). *Caveat:* tested on official statistics, not price forecasts.
  • Use one fixed denominator and show both forms: "100번 중 38번 (38%)". Never "1 in X". 1-in-n accuracy 45% vs 55–57% (Cuite 2008); 1-in-X inflates perceived risk (Sirota 2018). *Caveat:* frequency alone is not better than percent — percent won on absolute differences 72% vs 43% (Woloshin & Schwartz 2011) — hence both.
  • Keep the card to a few numbers; put detail behind a tap. "Increased detail often meant poorer performance" (EC KID 2015); less-is-more for low numeracy (Peters 2007); more information raises confidence more than accuracy (Tsai 2008); the median retail investor spends ~6 minutes per trade and many stop at the snapshot (Laarits & Wurgler 2025). *Caveat:* simplification may save time without changing choices (Beshears et al.: no effect of the Summary Prospectus).
  • Put the track record next to the claim, with both the stated hit rate and visible recent hits and misses. Trust follows observed accuracy as well as stated accuracy (Yin 2019). *Caveat:* seeing an algorithm err lowers use even when it beat the human 83% of the time (Dietvorst 2015) — show the comparison baseline in the same line, and expect lower uptake; that is the intended calibration.
  • Keep AI prose short and make its uncertainty first-person and tied to measured confidence. First-person uncertainty cut agreement 80.9% → 74.8% and raised accuracy 63.9% → 72.8% (Kim 2024); longer explanations raise confidence without accuracy (Steyvers 2025). *Caveat:* medical Q&A; trust intention also fell (3.25 → 2.91); generic hedges had no significant effect.
  • Do not rely on explanation to prevent over-trust; show the case against as well. Explanations raised acceptance regardless of correctness (Bansal 2021); contrastive for/against text reduced over-reliance (Si 2023); explanations help only when they make checking cheaper (Vasconcelos 2023). *Caveat:* designs that force thinking reduce over-reliance most and are liked least (Buçinca 2021).
  • One valid, openable source per factual sentence — not a list. Citations raise trust; one equals five; irrelevant citations lower it; checking lowers trust (Ding 2025, N = 303). *Caveat:* citations raise trust even when users do not open them, so a wrong citation is worse than none.
  • If a graphic is used, make it countable (20–50 dots or a 100-icon array), not a fan or density curve. Dotplot decisions reached 97% of optimal, +5 points over no uncertainty (Fernandes 2018); 1.15× lower variance than density (Kay 2016); funnel-of-doubt and histogram lost to a table or line (EC KID 2015). *Caveat:* transit decisions with feedback; icon arrays make risk feel less serious (Galesic 2009); a plain table may be enough.
  • Replace "past performance does not guarantee" boilerplate with the specific base-rate fact. The standard disclaimer changed nothing; a statement that high returns generally do not persist worked (Mercer, Palmiter, Taha 2010). *Caveat:* one experiment on fund advertisements; no effect size in the abstract.
  • Keep the purchase price off the judgment panel. Hiding it shrank the disposition effect by 25% (Frydman & Rangel 2014). *Caveat:* 58 lab subjects; the bias remains.

Supplementary, weaker evidence: give the bad case as a numeric range with a reference point, because the size of a bare range barely registered (van der Bles Exp 2). For streaming, do not shorten latency at the cost of content — a 2 s first token was rated slightly less thoughtful than 9–20 s and trust did not move (Tan 2026); there is no evidence either way on sentence-level reveal.

(b) Merging explanation + numbers + track record in one panel vs splitting

  • No direct finance experiment comparing one consolidated panel with several detailed panels was found (못 찾음). The conclusion below is by analogy.
  • For merging: a single structured table of outcomes produced large comprehension gains (70% vs 8%, drug facts box); decision aids that put options, probabilities and trade-offs in one artefact improve knowledge (+11.9/100) and risk perception (RR 1.94); simpler KID variants beat detailed ones; retail attention is ~6 minutes, mostly on one snapshot page.
  • Against merging everything at equal weight: fluent explanation placed beside numbers raises confidence without accuracy (Steyvers) and raises acceptance of wrong answers (Bansal). More content raises confidence faster than accuracy (Tsai).
  • Reading: one panel, numbers first and fixed, track record on the same panel as the claim it qualifies, prose short and subordinate, everything else on demand. The evidence supports merging the three *kinds* of content, not merging their full detail.

What this means for card wording (my reading, not legal advice)

  • The statutory triggers are "대가를 받고" and "업/영업으로". A free, non-commercial tool for the owner and friends does not obviously meet them, but that is an inference, not something a source states.
  • Whatever the status, the wording that the statute and the regulator single out is the wording to avoid: guaranteed profit or loss cover; returns that were not realised; a track record that shows only the good stretch; stating an uncertain outcome as certain; advice framed for one named person's situation ("you should sell now").
  • Wording that stays on the information side: past-frequency statements with the period and sample size, a range instead of a point target, the miss shown with the hit rate, a loss-is-yours line, and an AI-generated label on streamed sentences.
  • Press analysis (요약만) puts the risk line at using a person's holdings, risk profile or trading pattern to suggest timing or weights. A card that reads the user's own position to phrase a buy/sell prompt sits closer to that line than one that describes the asset.

심사자가 받아들일 평가 설계

  • 컷오프 이후만 채점 — 사용 모델의 지식 컷오프 뒤 날짜만 시험 구간으로 쓴다(G1). 가능하면 시점 일치 모델(G2 · G3 계열)로 과거 구간을 보강하되, 한국어 지원 여부는 확인해야 한다(미확인).
  • 누출 직접 측정 — 종목명 익명화 대조(G5)와 LAP 검정(G4)을 같이 보고한다. 컷오프 전 · 후 성능 차이를 표로 낸다.
  • 사전 등록 전진 시험 — 가설 · 지평 · 임계 · 통과 기준을 먼저 적고 라이브로 쌓는다. 과거 검산은 보조 근거.
  • 매매 가능한 시점 — 뉴스 · 공시의 첫 공개 시각을 쓰고, 진입은 그 뒤 첫 5분봉 종가 또는 다음 날 시가. 초기 반응(매매 불가 구간)은 성과에서 뺀다(G1 이 둘을 분리해 보고). A4-6 처럼 1분 안에 0.92% 가 반영되므로 이 구분이 결과를 좌우한다.
  • 비용 민감도 표 — 왕복 0 · 10 · 20 · 30bp 와 한국의 실제 증권거래세 · 수수료 · 호가 스프레드를 넣은 순수익. 손익분기 비용을 적는다(G1 은 20bp).
